# Revenue-state cohort analysis through April 2026

This analysis is generated by `sql/cohort_state_analysis.sql`. It follows each
activation cohort across customer age and excludes provisional May data.

For the consolidated presentation terminology and KPI selection, see
`FINAL_PRESENTATION_KPI_FRAMEWORK.md`.

## State definitions

| State | Operational definition | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Never monetized | Activated, but no revenue row has appeared by the observation month | Activation did not yet translate into monetization. This is not churn because the company was never revenue-active. |
| Active | Revenue row in the observation month | Revenue-active in that month. There are no exactly-zero total-revenue rows through April, so row presence and non-zero revenue are equivalent here. |
| At-risk | Previously active, then absent for one or two consecutive months | Approximately 30-60 days without revenue using monthly data. |
| Churned proxy | Previously active, then absent for at least three consecutive months | Approximately 90+ days without revenue. It is a behavioral proxy, not confirmed customer churn, and a company can still return. |

The thresholds are supported by the observed probability of returning within
the next two months: 13.4% after one inactive month, 8.1% after two, 4.9% after
three, and 0% after four among the fully observable spells. The four-month
sample is only 30 spells, so the threshold should be re-estimated with more data.

`company_closed_date` is not used in these states. Closure and revenue inactivity
are related but distinct events.

## Cohort design

- Cohort: calendar month of activation.
- Age 0: activation month; age 1: the following calendar month, and so on.
- Observation window: October 2025 through April 2026.
- Denominator: all companies activated in the cohort, including those that
  never monetize.
- Segments: overall, persona, initial subscription group, and their intersection.
- Revenue per company: monthly revenue divided by the original activated cohort,
  not only the companies active in that month.

The fair comparison is across cohorts at the same age. A cohort's latest
available state should not be compared directly with a newer cohort because the
older cohort has had more time to become inactive.

The state profile is stable: age-three Active share stays within a narrow

revenue per activated company is lower for the December and January cohorts,
however, so stable activity does not imply stable monetization intensity.

### Persona highlights

Among personas with at least 100 age-three observations:

- `Bikers_Drivers` has the clearest risk signal: 80.46% Active, 11.55% At-risk,
  and 7.28% Churned proxy across 563 companies.
- BTP combines scale and value: 1,642 companies, 90.50% Active, 2.68% Churned
  proxy, and EUR 57.00 monthly revenue per original activated company.
- Wholesale has the highest monthly revenue per company in this filtered view
  at EUR 63.17, but only 210 observations.

## State-transition evidence

Across cohorts at the same ages:

- About 3%-4% of Active companies move to At-risk in a typical next month.
- At age 2, 13.36% of At-risk companies return to Active; the recovery rate is
  7.21% at age 3 and about 5% at ages 4-5.
- Once classified Churned proxy, only 3.30% return at age 4, 3.96% at age 5,
  and 0.82% at age 6. Later-age samples are progressively smaller.

This supports using At-risk as the intervention window. It also supports keeping
the label `Churned proxy`: recovery is uncommon but not impossible.

## Suggested case takeaway

> Lifecycle health is stable across comparable activation cohorts, but risk is
> concentrated in Business and Bikers/Drivers. Target reactivation during the
> first two inactive months, before the observed return probability falls below
> 5% after the third inactive month.

## Questions for the interviewers

1. Does a missing revenue row mean zero activity, or can it reflect delayed or
   incomplete ingestion?
2. Should activity be defined by any recognized revenue, transactions, account
   login, balance, or another product-use event?
3. Are subscription plan changes available? The current segmentation uses the
   initial plan only.
4. Is there a formal churn event or cancellation reason that can validate the
   90-day revenue-inactivity proxy?
5. Is May's signup extraction incomplete, and should future months be added to
   validate return probabilities and mature the newer cohorts?
