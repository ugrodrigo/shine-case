# Shine France: external market research and revised strategy

**Research date:** 30 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Pressure-test the case-study findings against Shine's current
business model, competitive environment, the French BTP market, and regulatory
change.

## Executive conclusion

The supplied dataset shows that **BTP is Shine's strongest demonstrated
persona**, not that it is automatically the largest untapped market.

External research supports BTP product fit: France has a large, predominantly
small-business construction base, and Shine already offers BTP-specific
invoicing, expense-management, collection, and insurance workflows. It also
reveals meaningful constraints: construction activity is contracting, Shine
cannot provide an overdraft directly, advanced construction invoicing is only
partly supported, and Shine is already actively targeting BTP. The observed BTP
performance could therefore reflect an existing acquisition strategy or
customer selection rather than unused market headroom.

The strategic recommendation is revised to:

1. **Run a bounded BTP-fit activation and monetization experiment**, focused on
   independent tradespeople and small crews whose needs match Shine's product.
   Use invoicing, payment collection, expense controls, cash-flow signals, and
   relevant partner services as candidate levers. Do not assume that acquiring
   any BTP company or moving it to Business will create value.
2. **Protect high-value accounts at material revenue decline and first revenue
   gap**, retaining the dataset-led early-warning initiative.
3. Treat **electronic invoicing as a cross-persona distribution and retention
   mechanism**, initially tested within BTP and expanded only if it improves
   activation, account health, or incremental revenue versus control.

The current Business initial-plan group remains a secondary niche, not a lead
growth bet. Its high average revenue is based on a small and comparatively
unhealthy population, and the dataset contains initial plan rather than current
plan history.

## 1. What the dataset establishes

Across the 9,088-company equal-tenure comparable base:

- BTP contributes **26.74% of revenue** from 1,642 companies and generates
  **1.48 times** overall average revenue per company.
- Plus contributes **28.56% of plan revenue** at **2.02 times** average revenue
  per company.
- Start contributes **44.64%** through scale, at approximately average revenue
  per company.
- Business produces **3.22 times** average revenue per company but contributes
  only **6.95%**, with just 196 comparable companies.
- In the mature base, BTP is 71.07% Healthy and 15.55% Watch. Business is only
  46.15% Healthy, with 43.36% inactive, based on 143 mature companies.

These are associations within Shine's extracted population. They do not show
market size, customer acquisition cost, current plan, contribution margin,
competitive switching, or causal product effects.

## 2. Shine's current business model

Shine is an ACPR-authorised **payment institution**, not a traditional bank.
It provides a French IBAN, cards, transfers, collections, invoicing, accounting
integrations, and administrative tools. It cannot directly offer an authorised
overdraft, and some credit services depend on partners.

Its visible revenue model combines:

- subscription tiers: Free, Start, Plus, and Business;
- transaction and usage fees, including foreign payments, cash deposits,
  additional operations, and card-based invoice collection;
- increasing product depth through invoicing, accounting, expense management,
  insurance, and financing partnerships.

Current headline prices are Free at EUR 0, Start at EUR 9, Plus at EUR 20, and
Business at EUR 60 per month excluding tax, with billing conditions and annual
discounts depending on the offer. Business is explicitly positioned for teams
and includes ten premium physical cards, unlimited virtual cards, team access,
and the highest quotas.

In January 2026, Shine announced that it had joined Ageras. The combined
direction is a European small-business platform integrating payments,
accounting, invoicing, payroll, and tax administration. This supports a broader
workflow strategy, rather than competing only as a current account.

**Sources:** [Shine pricing](https://www.shine.fr/tarifs/),
[difference from a traditional bank](https://help.shine.fr/fr/articles/1175387-quelles-sont-les-differences-entre-shine-et-une-banque-dite-traditionnelle),
[overdraft limitation and financing partners](https://help.shine.fr/fr/articles/1179200-compte-a-decouvert),
and [Shine joining Ageras](https://help.shine.fr/fr/articles/10289941-shine-rejoint-ageras).

## 3. Competitive pressure

Public prices are indicative and not perfectly comparable because included
cards, transfers, billing periods, support, and product scope differ.

| Provider | Indicative public pricing | Main competitive angle |
|---|---:|---|
| Shine | Free; Start EUR 9; Plus EUR 20; Business EUR 60 | French support, invoicing, administration, BTP proposition |
| Qonto | Basic EUR 9; Smart EUR 19; Premium EUR 39 | Accounting integration, cash-flow tools, expense management |
| Indy | Core account free; Premium from EUR 12 | Free account integrated with accounting and declarations |
| Revolut Business | Basic EUR 10; Grow EUR 35; Scale EUR 125 | International payments, foreign exchange, team controls |

The important strategic implication is not that one provider is universally
cheaper. It is that basic banking and electronic invoicing are becoming highly
competitive. Shine must win through a more complete workflow, service quality,
or vertical relevance.

**Sources:** [Qonto pricing](https://qonto.com/fr/pricing),
[Indy professional account](https://www.indy.fr/compte-pro/), and
[Revolut Business pricing](https://www.revolut.com/fr-FR/business/business-account-plans/).

## 4. BTP market: substantial but not unlimited

### Why BTP can scale

- INSEE records **650,207 economically active construction legal entities** in
  France in 2024.
- The FFB reports that approximately **95% of building businesses are
  artisan-sized**, close to Shine's small-business target profile.
- Shine already provides a BTP-specific proposition covering mobile account
  management, invoices and reminders, expense cards, accounting exports,
  cheque and cash collections, and partner construction insurance.
- BTP customers in the supplied data combine meaningful population scale with
  above-average Shine revenue.

### What caps the opportunity

- Artisan building activity fell **3.8% in 2025** and remained down **1.5%
  year-on-year in Q1 2026**.
- Construction formations fell **3.8% in 2025**, while French business
  formation overall grew to a record level.
- Shine does not directly provide an authorised overdraft. Partner financing
  mitigates but does not eliminate the working-capital limitation.
- Shine states that its invoicing product may not suit advanced progress
  billing, systematic retention guarantees, public contracts, or specialised
  BTP item structures.
- Shine already has a dedicated BTP acquisition page. Strong performance could
  reflect past targeting, channel mix, or selection rather than a new market.

The best-fit BTP target is therefore **an independent tradesperson or small
crew, digitally operated, with relatively simple projects and moderate direct
financing needs**. Larger contractors, long-project businesses, public-works
specialists, and companies dependent on overdrafts are a less certain fit.

[FFB building-sector figures](https://www.ffbatiment.fr/le-batiment-en-chiffres),
[CAPEB Q1 2026 outlook](https://www.capeb.fr/www/capeb/media/national/note-1-trimestre-2026-v3.pdf),
[INSEE 2025 company creation](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/8721354),

## 5. Cross-persona market opportunity

France recorded **1.166 million company creations in 2025**, including about
**759,000 micro-entrepreneurs**. Construction accounted for 85,600 creations
but declined 3.8%; faster-growing sectors included commerce and information and
communication. This argues against treating BTP as the only growth route.

Electronic invoicing is the clearest immediate external trigger:

- from **1 September 2026**, all French businesses must be able to receive
  electronic invoices;
- from **1 September 2027**, SMEs and microbusinesses must issue them
  electronically;
- Shine is an officially registered electronic-invoicing platform and includes
  the service in every plan, including Free.

Compliance alone is unlikely to be a durable differentiator because competitors
also offer electronic invoicing. Its value is as an entry point into repeated
workflows: receiving and issuing invoices, collecting payment, reconciling
transactions, monitoring cash flow, and preparing accounting.

**Sources:** [French Ministry of Economy reform calendar](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/tout-savoir-sur-la-facturation-electronique-pour-les-entreprises),
[official platform information](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/facturation-electronique-et-plateformes-agreees), and
[Shine accredited-platform confirmation](https://help.shine.fr/fr/articles/11459160-shine-plateforme-agreee-de-facturation-electronique).

## 6. Revised ranked recommendation

### Rank 1: BTP-fit activation and monetization experiment

**Position:** Treat BTP as the best demonstrated vertical in the dataset and a
test bed for a repeatable workflow, not as an automatically scalable
acquisition audience.

**Candidate intervention:** For eligible BTP companies, offer a coherent setup
journey around electronic invoicing, payment collection, expense cards and
controls, tax or project subaccounts, cash-flow alerts, and appropriate
insurance or short-term-financing partners. The exact treatment should be
chosen only after product-usage data identifies the adoption gap.

**Target population:** Independent tradespeople and small crews whose current
usage and operational needs match the product. Persona alone must not determine
eligibility or adverse treatment.

**Test:** Randomise eligible BTP accounts between the vertical journey and
business-as-usual. Measure incremental mean recognised revenue per eligible
company over 90 days, together with activation, feature adoption, account
health, complaints, support demand, closures, fraud/AML outcomes, and fairness
guardrails.

**Expansion rule:** Scale within BTP only if incremental contribution exceeds
fully loaded treatment and servicing cost. Expand the proven workflow to other
personas only after testing transferability.

### Rank 2: revenue-decline and first-gap protection

Retain the existing behavior-based initiative. A material revenue decline
should trigger diagnosis; the first missing-revenue month should trigger
appropriately governed support. This is more defensible than calling company
closure or one revenue gap customer churn.

### Business plan position

Do not make Business acquisition a lead initiative. The internal sample is
small and weak on continuity, the current offer is a EUR 60 team product, and
competition is strong. Test Business selectively among firms with observable
team and expense-management needs. Obtain effective-dated current-plan history
before attributing any effect to the plan.

## 7. What to validate before leadership acts

1. **Acquisition and selection:** BTP acquisition channel, historical campaign
   spend, application and KYB approval rates, conversion, CAC, and payback.
2. **Product fit:** current plan, migrations, invoicing, subaccounts, cards,
   team access, collections, financing requests, rejected payments, and whether
   Shine is the primary or a secondary account.
3. **Unit economics:** gross margin by revenue type, partner economics, support
   and operational cost, incremental contribution, and retention by segment.
4. **Persona governance:** exact BTP definition and provenance, stability over
   time, permitted KYB use, privacy controls, and small-cell suppression.
5. **Causal evidence:** pre-register the intervention, use random assignment,
   and separate association from incremental impact.

## Presentation-ready wording

> BTP is Shine's strongest demonstrated vertical, not yet a proven untapped
> market. Its large artisan base and strong workflow fit support a targeted
> experiment, while sector contraction, financing constraints, advanced
> invoicing needs, and existing BTP marketing limit broad extrapolation. We
> recommend testing a BTP-specific activation and health journey, using
> electronic invoicing as the workflow entry point, and scaling only when
> incremental contribution and guardrails outperform business-as-usual.
