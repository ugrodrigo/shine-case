# Revenue streaks and loyalty through April 2026

This analysis is generated by `sql/streak_analysis.sql`. May is excluded.

The formal presentation definition of a Healthy revenue account is consolidated
in `docs/presentation/FINAL_PRESENTATION_KPI_FRAMEWORK.md` and implemented in
`sql/presentation_kpis.sql`.

## What a streak means here

A revenue streak is the number of consecutive calendar months with a revenue
row after activation. There are no exactly-zero total-revenue rows through
April, so a revenue row also means non-zero net revenue in this extract.

This is a measure of **revenue consistency**, not confirmed emotional loyalty,
product satisfaction, or contractual retention.

- Continuous: revenue in every month from activation through the observation
  month.
- Restarted: active now, but at least one earlier month was missing.
- Current streak: consecutive active months since the latest missing month.
- Maximum streak: longest observed active run up to that point.

The streak and inactivity-state metrics complement each other:

- Streaks describe positive continuity while a company is active.
- At-risk and Churned proxy describe the duration of the current inactivity.

## Do streaks add information?

### For the headline cohort distribution: only a little

| Customer age | Active | Continuously active | Active after restarting | Difference between Active and continuous |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 95.72% | 95.62% | 0.09% | 0.10 pp |
| 2 | 92.29% | 91.67% | 0.62% | 0.62 pp |
| 3 | 89.88% | 88.88% | 1.00% | 1.00 pp |
| 4 | 88.02% | 86.74% | 1.28% | 1.28 pp |
| 5 | 86.20% | 84.66% | 1.54% | 1.54 pp |
| 6 | 83.61% | 81.94% | 1.67% | 1.67 pp |

Reactivation is rare, so Active share and continuous-streak share are almost the
same. A separate streak chart would therefore add little to a three-slide case.

### As a predictive or early-warning feature: yes

Among active observations at customer ages 2-4 with a following month visible:

| Streak type | Active company-months | Active next month | Mean current revenue | Median current revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Continuous since activation | 17,106 | 97.32% | EUR 47.68 | EUR 17.70 |
| Restarted after a gap | 164 | 78.66% | EUR 20.35 | EUR 11.01 |

The gap is 18.66 percentage points in next-month activity. The restarted group
also has lower current revenue on both the mean and median. This suggests that a
broken streak is useful as an early-warning feature even after a company returns.

However, the restarted sample is small and contains repeated company-months.
Treat this as descriptive evidence, not a causal estimate or a production model.

- Start is the best initial plan for loyalty at scale.

- These are initial plans, not necessarily the companies' current plans.

## Recommendation for the case

Do not make streaks a separate headline KPI. Use one supporting insight:

> A broken revenue streak remains informative even after reactivation: restarted
> companies were active the following month 78.7% of the time versus 97.3% for
> continuously active companies at comparable observed ages 2-4.

Operationally, combine the signals:

1. Active with an unbroken streak: healthy.
2. Active after restarting: recovered, but monitor closely.
3. At-risk after one or two inactive months: intervention window.
4. Churned proxy after three inactive months: low-probability recovery pool.

For segment prioritization, BTP is the balanced growth segment, Consultant is
the strongest large-persona loyalty segment, and Bikers Drivers is the clearest
retention intervention segment.

## Limitations

- Only seven confirmed monthly observations are available, so the maximum
  possible streak is seven months. This is too short to claim long-term loyalty.
- Any non-zero revenue counts as active; the metric does not distinguish EUR 1
  from EUR 100 of revenue.
- Monthly data cannot identify activity within a month or exact day-based streaks.
- Segment comparisons may reflect acquisition mix, seasonality, or product mix.
- More months and transaction or product-usage events would make a much stronger
  loyalty metric.
