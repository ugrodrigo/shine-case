# Shine case: simple raw-data memo

**Purpose:** A presentation memo that can be reproduced from the two base
tables with straightforward SQL. It keeps the advanced modelling as a clearly
labelled validation layer rather than making it look like the starting point.

**Confirmed cutoff:** April 2026. May is retained only as a provisional
sensitivity because signup coverage is incomplete.

## Page 1 — The raw-data big picture

### Position

> BTP is the largest revenue persona across the full confirmed period through
> April. Start supplies the most revenue through scale,
> while Plus and Business generate more revenue per revenue-producing company.
> The immediate opportunity is to test better BTP product adoption; the main
> measurable risk is revenue deterioration among accounts that are still
> producing revenue.

### Headline raw-data evidence

- Confirmed cumulative revenue through April is **EUR 2.35m**, generated by
  **17,568** companies at least once during the period.
- BTP contributes **25.42% of confirmed cumulative revenue**. Its **24.06% of
  April revenue** is retained as a current-snapshot cross-check.
- Start contributes **44.49% of confirmed cumulative revenue** through scale.
- Plus contributes **26.98%** at EUR 279.26 cumulative revenue per
  revenue-producing company.
- Business contributes only **6.78%**, despite EUR 458.05 cumulative revenue per
  revenue-producing company; only 348 Business companies generated confirmed
  revenue during the period.
- Interchange contributes **42.0%** of April revenue and deposit interest
  **28.7%**. Together they represent **70.7%**, so usage and balances matter
  more than subscriptions alone.

### Simple interpretation

BTP is the clearest large persona to test. Start is the scale engine. Plus is a
credible efficiency signal. Business is high-value per active company but too
small to justify becoming the lead growth strategy from this data alone.

The cumulative view provides the big picture but gives older companies more
months to generate revenue. The April snapshot avoids that particular exposure
problem, but it represents only one month. Their similar rankings are
reassuring, not a substitute for a controlled tenure comparison.

## Page 2 — Two ranked actions

Visuals on this page show the monthly recognized-revenue trend with May marked
as provisional, and the simple March-to-April decline/gap screen. The latter is
a diagnostic population view, not a churn chart.

### 1. Test BTP-fit activation and monetization

Run a controlled test for suitable BTP companies around a specific product
workflow, such as invoicing and collection, expense cards, cash-flow alerts, or
relevant partner services. Do not assume the initial plan itself causes higher
revenue.

Why first:

- BTP leads both the April and cumulative confirmed raw views.
- It combines meaningful population scale with above-average revenue per
  revenue-producing company.
- Shine already has a BTP proposition, making a bounded product experiment more
  actionable than an undefined acquisition campaign.

What cannot be sized from the raw data:

- Incremental revenue from the intervention.
- CAC, margin, servicing cost, payback, or current market penetration.
- Which BTP accounts have the product need or are already using the feature.

### 2. Diagnose material declines and the first revenue gap

A simple March-to-April check finds:

- **13,189** companies generated revenue in both months.
- **1,543 (11.70%)** remained revenue-active but fell by at least 30% and EUR 10.
- **424** generated March revenue but had no April revenue row.

Use these as diagnostic populations, not as confirmed churn. A two-month
comparison is vulnerable to normal volatility, seasonality, delayed posting,
and one-off transactions. It tells the team where to investigate, not which
customers have left.

### Alternatives not selected

- **Acquire more Business accounts:** high revenue per active company, but a
  small population and no CAC, current-plan, or product-need data.
- **Immediate plan upsell:** initial plan is an association, not a causal lever.
- **Broad churn campaign:** neither closure nor one missing revenue month proves
  churn.
- **Use May as the headline:** the revenue exists, but incomplete signup coverage
  makes comparisons with earlier months unsafe.

## Page 3 — What is simple, what is advanced, and how to prove it

### Base-table definitions

- `companies` is the typed copy of the raw companies CSV.
- `revenue_with_total` is the typed revenue CSV with the four revenue columns
  added into `total_revenue`.
- A **revenue company** is a company with a revenue row in the selected period.
- The raw cumulative view includes every confirmed revenue row through April.
- The April view includes April revenue only.

### Why keep the advanced layer

| Question | Simple memo approach | More rigorous validation |
|---|---|---|
| Which persona is largest? | April and cumulative `GROUP BY persona` | Equal-tenure first-three-complete-month ranking in `sql/revenue_concentration_analysis.sql` |
| Is revenue concentrated? | Not used as the primary segment rule | Top-10/top-20 and Pareto sensitivities in `sql/revenue_concentration_analysis.sql` |
| Is an account healthy? | March-to-April decline or missing row | Trailing-median health states in `sql/presentation_kpis.sql` |
| Is inactivity likely permanent? | One missing month is a flag | Return curves and lifecycle states in `sql/kpi_deep_dive.sql` |
| Do cohorts behave differently? | Not answered | Equal-age cohort states in `sql/cohort_state_analysis.sql` |
| Do streaks matter? | Not answered | Persistence and restart analysis in `sql/streak_analysis.sql` |

The advanced analyses do not replace the raw numbers. They test whether the
simple story survives fairer exposure, stronger definitions, and sensitivity
checks. The important result is that BTP remains first in the equal-tenure
ranking: **26.74% of comparable revenue**, versus **24.06% in April** and
**25.42% in the raw confirmed cumulative view**.

### Lead experiment

- Define eligibility from current product need, not persona alone.
- Randomly assign eligible companies to the BTP workflow or business-as-usual.
- Primary metric: incremental recognized revenue per eligible company over 90
  days.
- Guardrails: closures, complaints, support demand, fraud/AML outcomes,
  operational failures, and fairness.
- Scale only if incremental contribution exceeds the fully loaded intervention
  cost without material guardrail deterioration.

### Validate first

1. Confirm that a missing revenue row means zero rather than delayed or missing
   ingestion.
2. Obtain current plan, product usage, acquisition channel, CAC, margin, and
   servicing cost.
3. Confirm the BTP persona definition and permitted use of KYB-derived data.

## Page 4 — Revenue-component heatmap

The heatmap shows cumulative subscription, interchange, banking-fee, and
deposit-interest revenue per revenue company for every persona through April.
Each cell divides the cumulative revenue component by the number of distinct
companies with at least one revenue row during the confirmed period. Darker
cells therefore indicate more cumulative euros per such company.

The neutral **Total** column on the right is the sum of the four components and
is used to order personas from highest to lowest cumulative revenue per revenue
company. It is excluded from the heatmap colour scale because its systematically
higher values would compress the component colours and make their differences
harder to see.

Use the Page 1 bars to judge segment scale and this heatmap to understand
monetization intensity and revenue drivers. The chart does not replace the
scale view: a small persona can have a high average without representing a
large total opportunity.

### Limitations

- This is cumulative revenue per revenue company, not a monthly average.
  Companies with longer observation periods have had more time to accumulate
  revenue, so tenure can influence the ranking.
- The inner join excludes companies with no revenue row. The denominator is
  therefore revenue companies, not all signed-up or activated companies.
- Small personas can produce unstable averages. Repair Installation has only
  38 revenue companies and should not be treated as a proven scalable segment.
- Revenue is not contribution margin, and the heatmap shows association rather
  than evidence that persona membership caused higher monetization.

## Page 5 — Initial-plan revenue-component heatmap

This page repeats the cumulative revenue-per-revenue-company view by
`initial_subscription_group`. The four revenue components use the heatmap
colour scale. The neutral **Total** column is their sum, is excluded from that
scale, and orders the plans from highest to lowest.

The company count beside each plan is essential context. A plan can have high
cumulative revenue per revenue company while representing a relatively small
population and a limited share of total revenue.

### Limitations

- `initial_subscription_group` is the plan at the initial observation and may
  not be the company's current plan. Plan migrations are unavailable.
- The metric is cumulative rather than monthly, so company tenure can affect
  the differences between plans.
- The inner join excludes companies without a revenue row from the denominator.
- The comparison is associative. Company size, age, selection, and behaviour
  may explain the differences; it is not evidence that moving a company to a
  higher plan would cause its revenue to increase.

## Page 6 — Full-period revenue-based account health

This page adds one advanced, self-contained analysis whose only source tables
are `companies` and `revenue_with_total`. It builds the missing company-month
calendar inside the query, calculates a rolling three-month revenue baseline,
tracks prior gaps and inactivity, and assigns a revenue-health state for every
confirmed monthly snapshot from October 2025 through April 2026.

At each month-end, the model first asks whether an activated company produced
revenue. Active accounts are then separated by momentum and continuity;
inactive accounts are separated by the length of their revenue gap. These are
behavioural signals, not contractual customer statuses.

### Definitions

- **Healthy:** revenue-active, has three complete post-activation baseline
  months, has no prior post-revenue gap, and has no material decline against
  its prior three-month median.
- **Watch:** revenue-active, but at least 30% and EUR 10 below the prior
  three-month median.
- **Active — building history:** revenue-active but does not yet have the three
  complete baseline months required for a Healthy/Watch momentum assessment.
- **Recovered:** revenue-active after a gap that occurred after first revenue.
- **At-risk:** previously monetized and absent for one or two months.
- **Churned proxy:** previously monetized and absent for at least three months.
- **Never monetized:** no revenue observed to date in the extract.

### Comparison design

- The October-April trend uses the fixed 1,196-company October activation
  cohort. This prevents the trend from changing merely because newer cohorts
  enter later.
- The April persona and initial-plan views use all 6,337 companies activated by
  December 2025. Every company therefore has three complete post-activation
  months available for the momentum baseline.
- October-January are shown as part of the lifecycle, but revenue-active
  companies are labelled Building history rather than Healthy or Watch until
  the required baseline exists. May remains excluded because it is provisional.

### April snapshot

- Healthy: **74.77%**.
- Watch: **10.62%**.
- At-risk plus Churned proxy: **12.91%**.
- Business is the clearest initial-plan warning: only **46.15% Healthy** and
  **53.85% combined Watch + At-risk + Churned proxy**, on a small base of 143.
- BTP is more balanced at **71.07% Healthy**, but its combined risk signal is
  still approximately **28%** across 1,151 comparable companies.

### Limitations

This remains a revenue-health proxy. It does not measure product use,
satisfaction, profitability, account status, or confirmed cancellation. The
query assumes that a missing revenue row means no observed monthly revenue; that
must be validated before operational use. Historical interpretation is also
left-censored by the October extract start, and initial plan may not be current.
The activation month is a calendar month with potentially partial exposure, so
continuity involving that month should be interpreted cautiously.
