# Revenue concentration through April 2026

This analysis is generated by `sql/revenue_concentration_analysis.sql`. May is
excluded from all calculations.

## Definition

The main view ranks the 17,568 companies with at least one revenue row by their
cumulative total revenue from October through April. The top 10% is the first
1,757 companies after sorting from highest to lowest revenue.

Because cumulative revenue favors older companies, a second sensitivity ranks
companies by cumulative revenue through customer age three. Every company in
that sensitivity has exactly four possible revenue months: ages 0-3.

The preferred **concentration sensitivity** excludes the partial activation
month and uses ages 1-3: the first three complete calendar months after
activation. The source revenue table is monthly, so it cannot divide age-0
revenue precisely at the activation date. An exact 90-day window would require
daily revenue data. This is secondary context; segment ranking uses all 9,088
comparable companies rather than only the top 20%.

## Tenure bias and age-three sensitivity

Cumulative ranking is strongly affected by exposure time:

| Activation cohort | Share entering cumulative top decile |
|---|---:|
| October 2025 | 24.29% |

| January 2026 | 9.74% |
| February 2026 | 5.77% |
| March 2026 | 2.39% |

For this reason, cumulative membership should not be interpreted as customer
quality without a tenure control.

In the age-three sensitivity, 909 of 9,088 comparable activated companies form
the top decile and generate 49.61% of revenue through age three. The main
segment conclusions remain consistent:

- Business representation: 4.39x; Plus: 2.48x; Start: 0.90x; Free: 0.40x.
- BTP representation: 1.61x and 31.94% of top-decile revenue.
- Automobile Trade Repair: 1.59x; Restauration: 1.46x; Wholesale: 1.33x.

The drop from 56.61% to 49.61% shows that tenure explains some concentration,
but roughly half of comparable four-month revenue still comes from 10% of
companies.

### Preferred method: first three complete months

Excluding activation month retains the same 9,088 companies while giving every

The segment result remains stable:

- BTP: 482 companies, 26.51% of the top 20%, 30.28% of its revenue, 1.47x

- BTP + Plus: 170 companies, 9.35% of the top 20%, 10.47% of its revenue, and
  2.44x represented.

### Presentation-ready takeaway

> The highest-value 20% of comparable companies generate 70% of revenue across
> the first three complete post-activation months. BTP represents 26% of this
> pool and 30% of its revenue, while Plus
> and Business companies are respectively 2.2x and 3.6x overrepresented.

This is close to a Pareto-style pattern but should be described as `70/20`, not
as a literal `80/20` rule. The cumulative, non-tenure-controlled sensitivity is
stronger at `74/20`.

## Does the top 20% satisfy the Pareto rule?

No. The concentration curve is:

| Top share of companies | Cumulative through April | Tenure-controlled through age 3 |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1% | 17.52% | 14.38% |
| 5% | 41.08% | 35.19% |
| 10% | 56.61% | 49.61% |
| 20% | **73.93%** | **67.02%** |
| 30% | 84.08% | 77.99% |
| 50% | 94.70% | 91.05% |

The cumulative view reaches 80% of revenue at the top 25.47% of revenue
companies: 4,474 of 17,568. After giving every company the same four-month
exposure, 80% requires the top 32.30%: 2,935 of 9,088.

Therefore, `80/20` is not supported. A more accurate description is
approximately `74/20` cumulatively or `67/20` after tenure control. The latter
is the more defensible statement about structural customer concentration.

## Recommended interpretation

> Revenue is meaningfully concentrated but not dependent on a handful of single
> accounts. The highest-value 10% generate about half of tenure-controlled
> revenue, with BTP, Plus, and Business strongly overrepresented. Concentration
> is greatest in interchange and deposit interest, not subscription.

This supports differentiated retention and service for high-value companies,
while also highlighting dependence on usage and customer balances. It does not
mean Business should automatically be the acquisition priority: the earlier
analysis shows Business also has the weakest funnel and revenue continuity.

For a three-slide presentation, use `70/20` as a secondary concentration KPI
and targeted-protection lens. Use the full 9,088-company comparable population
for the primary persona and plan ranking. Keep top-10 and cumulative results in
the appendix as sensitivities.
